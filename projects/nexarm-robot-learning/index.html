NexArm: Robot Learning, Simulation, and Data Pipeline

Contributions to a LeRobot/Hiwonder NexArm fork: MuJoCo simulation, demonstrations, and evidence-first policy evaluation.

At a glance

Problem. An affordable arm needs more than a viewer before it can support robot-learning experiments: its action contract, visual observations, contact behavior, demonstrations, and evaluation must agree.

My role. I contributed to an existing LeRobot/Hiwonder NexArm fork, rather than claiming authorship of the platform or vendor firmware. My work focused on MuJoCo simulation support, data generation, policy-evaluation tooling, Rerun integration, and focused validation.

Technical decisions. The simulator keeps the physical follower’s six-feature LeRobot action interface, maps raw servo positions to MuJoCo actuators, renders front and wrist RGB cameras, uses detailed STL meshes only for visuals, and uses stable primitive collisions for physics.

Verified outcome. After a gripper-base collision repair, the scripted pick-and-place generator passed 10 of 10 deterministic seeds. This verifies the scripted task path and collision geometry; it is not a learned-policy success claim.

From action to evidence

The physical and simulated followers share the same external action names: shoulder pan, shoulder lift, elbow flex, wrist flex, wrist roll, and gripper position. A raw LeRobot action reaches the physical follower through the Hiwonder serial/firmware path, while the simulation converts the same raw values into radian or metre targets for MuJoCo position actuators. Keeping that boundary explicit allows a dataset or policy interface to be inspected without pretending that the simulator executes vendor firmware.

The scene includes a floor, a free red cube, a green target zone, and front and wrist cameras. Visual STL meshes are excluded from contact because their full collision shapes produced unstable overlaps; simplified boxes and capsules provide the physics interface instead. Explicit contact exclusions cover connected links while preserving relevant contacts.

Demonstrations, datasets, and evaluation

Scripted MuJoCo demonstrations use damped-least-squares inverse kinematics to move through approach, grasp, lift, transfer, release, and retreat waypoints. Accepted episodes record six raw state values, six raw actions, both camera streams, seeded resets, task traces, rejection reasons, and source-asset hashes. That makes the data generation process reproducible and distinguishes it from manually collected physical demonstrations, which remain a future collection path.

The repository also supports ACT-compatible training and rollout paths, plus a benchmark interface for running compatible checkpoints on identical seeded episodes. Rerun integration makes camera observations and state/action streams inspectable during teleoperation or rollout. I do not report a learned-policy result here because a retained compatible checkpoint and fixed-seed evaluation report are required before making that claim.

Testing and debugging

The work added and used focused checks for raw-to-control conversion, camera rendering, finite simulation stepping, action-feature consistency, gripper open direction, collision masks, and object contact. A visible manipulation failure led to inspection of an invisible collision box: the gripper-base collider had its dimensions assigned to the wrong axes. Correcting it to the measured mesh envelope removed the obstruction, and the scripted physical success gate accepted 10 of 10 deterministic seeds.

Limits and next work

The simulator is MuJoCo-first and is not firmware-in-the-loop. Its motor dynamics, camera calibration, and sim-to-real correspondence remain approximate; physical joint direction, zero position, timing, and contact behavior must be measured before a hardware performance claim. The next useful milestone is a retained policy checkpoint evaluated on fixed seeds, followed by a controlled comparison against the physical arm.
