    ACT is conditioned on images and joint state only; it does not read the task text. Each cycle it reads every camera once, encodes them together with the arm’s state, and predicts a chunk of 100 actions: joint and gripper targets for the next 3.33 seconds at 30 Hz. The NexArm then runs the whole chunk before the policy is called again.

    images 3 × 3×480×640 · state 6 → chunk 100 × 6

  2. Observe

    The front Logitech, the wrist camera and, on the 3-camera dataset, the top RealSense each deliver one RGB frame. The five arm joints plus the gripper give a six-number state. Images and state are normalized with the dataset’s mean and standard deviation (the backbone sees ImageNet statistics).

    observation.images.{front,wrist,top} 3×480×640 · observation.state 6

  3. ResNet18 per image

    One ImageNet-initialized ResNet18 is shared by all cameras. Its last stage leaves a 512 × 15 × 20 feature map (stride 32). A 1×1 convolution keeps 512 channels, a 2D sinusoidal embedding marks where each cell came from, and the map is flattened into 300 tokens per camera.

    3×480×640 → 512×15×20 → 300 × 512 per camera

  4. Transformer encoder

    The encoder sees one sequence: a style token z (all zeros at inference), the joint state projected to 512, and the 900 image tokens. Four post-norm layers with 8 heads and a 3200-wide feed-forward let every token attend to every other, so a gripper pixel in the wrist view can be related to a bowl in the front view and to the current joint angles.

    902 × 512 → 4 × self-attention → memory 902 × 512

  5. Decoder and action head

    One hundred learned queries, one for each future time step, go through a single decoder layer: self-attention among the queries and cross-attention into the encoder memory. A linear layer maps each query to six numbers, which are un-normalized into five joint targets and a gripper target.

    100 queries × 512 → Linear 512→6 → chunk 100 × 6

  6. Execute the whole chunk

    lerobot-nexarm keeps ACT’s default n_action_steps = 100 with no temporal ensembling: the arm follows all 100 targets open loop, one every 33 ms, then captures new frames. In the 3D view the dots ahead of the gripper are the part of the current chunk still to run; when they run out, the next cycle starts.

    100 actions × 33 ms = 3.33 s between observations

Architecture

ACT predicts actions in chunks instead of one at a time. Predicting 100 steps at once means a single mistake in one prediction is not compounded at every step, and it lets the policy represent a smooth motion rather than a series of corrections. The cost is that the arm moves without new observations for the length of the executed part of the chunk; on the NexArm that is the full 3.33 seconds.

Part Setting in lerobot-nexarm
Vision backbone ResNet18, ImageNet-1k weights, shared across cameras
Image tokens 1×1 conv to 512, 2D sine position embedding, 300 per 480×640 view
Encoder 4 layers, d_model 512, 8 heads, FFN 3200, ReLU, post-norm
Decoder 1 layer, 100 learned queries
Action head Linear 512 → 6
Latent 32-dimensional CVAE latent, KL weight 10
Normalization mean / std for state and actions, ImageNet stats for images
Chunk / executed 100 / 100 actions, no temporal ensembling

Training

The model is trained as a conditional VAE. A second, smaller transformer reads the current joint state and the true next 100 actions and compresses them into a 32-dimensional latent z. That latent becomes the first token of the main encoder, so the network can tell apart demonstrations that look the same but were performed differently. The KL term keeps z close to a unit Gaussian, which is why it can be replaced by zeros at inference.

Setting Value
Data Real teleoperated demonstrations, thanhkt/nexarm_stack_bowls
Held-out split last 10% of episodes per task
Effective batch 16 (batch 2 × 8 gradient-accumulation steps, sized for an 8 GB GPU)
Optimizer AdamW, lr 1e-5 for the transformer and the backbone, weight decay 1e-4
Steps 100,000, checkpoint and held-out loss every 5,000
Loss L1 on actions, masked past the episode end, + 10 · KL

Camera sets must match. The default dataset has front and wrist cameras only; a 3-camera recording adds the top view. A policy trained on two cameras needs --no-top-cam at rollout, and rollout.py stops if the checkpoint and the robot disagree.

Evaluation status

Not yet measured

No physical success rate has been published yet. Training reports only the L1 loss on held-out episodes, which does not say whether the arm actually stacks the bowls. The table and protocol below are the planned evaluation, not results.

Metric Value How it is measured
Stacking success rate — Fraction of trials that end with the bowls nested and released, judged from the front camera.
Sub-task success — Separate counts for grasp, lift, place and release, so a failure can be located.
Time to stack — Seconds from the first action to the gripper opening over the stack, successful trials only.
Held-out L1 — Mean L1 over normalized actions on the last 10% of episodes, logged every 5,000 steps.

Protocol

  1. Fixed bowl start poses drawn from the recorded workspace, the same list for every policy.
  2. Each trial starts from the robot’s ready pose with examples/nexarm/rollout.py and the checkpoint’s own camera set.
  3. A trial ends at success, at a collision, or after 60 seconds.
