  1. One control cycle

    TurboVLA reads the cameras, the joint state and the task instruction. DINOv3 encodes the images, a frozen BERT encodes the text, six interaction layers let each modality attend to the other, and a small decoder predicts 16 future actions. Only the first 8 are run, about a quarter of a second, before the policy sees the scene again.

    \hat{A}_t = \pi_\theta\big(I_t^{1..V},\ \ell,\ s_t\big) \in [-1, 1]^{16 \times 6}

    V camera views, the instruction ℓ and the six-number joint state go in; one forward pass gives 16 rows of five joint targets and a gripper target, still in normalized units.

    images V × 3×224×224 · text L tokens · state 6 → chunk 16 × 6

  2. Observe

    Every camera frame is resized to 224 × 224 by the DINOv3 image processor and normalized with ImageNet statistics. Views are ordered front, wrist, top; the top camera is used only when the dataset recorded it, and the order is saved in the checkpoint’s config. The joint state is min-max scaled to [-1, 1] with ranges saved at training time.

    \tilde{s} = 2\,\frac{s - s_{\min}}{s_{\max} - s_{\min}} - 1
    \tilde{I} = \big(\operatorname{resize}_{224}(I) - \mu_{\text{IN}}\big) \oslash \sigma_{\text{IN}}

    smin, smax come from stats_turbovla.json; μIN, σIN are the per-channel ImageNet mean and standard deviation.

    V = 2 or 3 views · state 6 in [-1, 1]

  3. DINOv3 vision encoder

    Each view goes through DINOv3 ViT-B/16 on its own, in bf16. A 224-pixel image is 14 × 14 patches of 16 pixels; the class token and the four register tokens are dropped, so every view gives 196 tokens of width 768. A residual MLP projects them to 256 and a learned view embedding says which camera they came from. The backbone is fine-tuned.

    x = \operatorname{DINOv3}(\tilde I)_{[5:]} \in \mathbb{R}^{196 \times 768}
    z = \operatorname{LN}\big(W_{\text{skip}}\,x + W_2\,\operatorname{GELU}(W_1 \operatorname{LN}(x))\big) + e_{\text{view}}
    N_v = V \cdot (224 / 16)^2 = 196\,V

    W1 is 768 → 1024 (dropout 0.1 after the GELU), W2 is 1024 → 256 and Wskip is 768 → 256 without bias. eview is one learned 256-vector per camera.

    V × 196 × 768 → V × 196 × 256 → 588 × 256 with 3 views

  4. BERT language encoder

    The instruction, for example “stack the bowls”, is tokenized with WordPiece and encoded by BERT-base, which stays frozen in eval mode. A linear layer maps its 768-wide outputs to 256. The tokenizer’s special tokens and punctuation split the text into sentences, and a block mask keeps each token’s later self-attention inside its own sentence.

    l = W_t\,\operatorname{BERT}(\ell) \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 256}
    M_{ij} = \begin{cases} \text{attend} & i, j \text{ in the same sentence} \\ \text{masked} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}

    Wt is 768 → 256 with Xavier initialization; sentences are delimited by [CLS], [SEP], “.” and “?”. Padding tokens are also removed from the image-to-text attention.

    L × 768 → Linear → L × 256

  5. Vision ↔ language interaction

    Six layers fuse the two streams. A bi-attention block computes one matrix of attention logits between image and text tokens and uses it in both directions: softmax over the text for the image update, softmax over the images for the text update. Residuals start from the layer-normed input, and each update is scaled by a LayerScale γ that starts at 10-4, so a fresh layer is close to the identity. A text-only transformer layer then refines the text with the sentence mask.

    \bar v = \operatorname{LN}(v),\quad \bar l = \operatorname{LN}(l),\quad S = \frac{(W_q \bar v)(W_k \bar l)^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_h}}
    v \leftarrow \bar v + \operatorname{DropPath}\big(\gamma_v \odot W_o^{v}\,\operatorname{softmax}_{\text{text}}(S)\,W_{vl}\,\bar l\big)
    l \leftarrow \bar l + \operatorname{DropPath}\big(\gamma_l \odot W_o^{l}\,\operatorname{softmax}_{\text{img}}(S^{\top})\,W_{vv}\,\bar v\big)
    l \leftarrow \operatorname{TransformerLayer}(l;\ M)

    4 heads with dh = 256 (inner width 1024), DropPath 0.1. The text layer is post-norm with 4 heads and a 1024-wide ReLU feed-forward. After layer 6 the streams are concatenated.

    (588 + L) × 256 → 6 × interaction → (588 + L) × 256

  6. State tokens

    The normalized joint state does not take part in the interaction. A small MLP turns it into two 256-wide tokens, each with its own learned position, and they are appended to the image and text tokens to form the decoder’s memory.

    [\,s^{(1)} ;\ s^{(2)}\,] = \operatorname{reshape}\big(W_b\,\operatorname{GELU}(W_a \operatorname{LN}(\tilde s))\big) + [\,p_1 ;\ p_2\,]
    m = \big[\,v ;\ l ;\ \operatorname{LN}(s^{(1)}) ;\ \operatorname{LN}(s^{(2)})\,\big]

    Wa is 6 → 256 (dropout 0.1 after the GELU), Wb is 256 → 512, reshaped to 2 × 256.

    6 → 2 × 256 · memory (588 + L + 2) × 256

  7. ACT-style action head

    Sixteen learned queries, one per future step, pass through three pre-norm decoder layers (d 256, 8 heads, feed-forward 2048, dropout 0.1): self-attention among the queries, cross-attention into the memory, then a feed-forward block. A three-layer MLP maps each query to six numbers and tanh keeps them in [-1, 1]. There is no CVAE: the output is one deterministic chunk.

    q \leftarrow q + \operatorname{SelfAttn}(\operatorname{LN}(q))
    q \leftarrow q + \operatorname{CrossAttn}(\operatorname{LN}(q),\ m)
    q \leftarrow q + \operatorname{FFN}(\operatorname{LN}(q))
    \hat a = \tanh\big(\operatorname{MLP}_{256 \to 512 \to 512 \to 6}(q)\big)

    The three updates repeat for each of the 3 layers. The MLP uses ReLU between its layers; there is no final LayerNorm after the last decoder layer.

    16 queries × 256 → 3 decoder layers → MLP → tanh → 16 × 6

  8. Run 8, then infer again

    The min-max statistics turn the chunk back into joint and gripper targets. rollout_turbovla.py runs the first 8 (--open-loop-steps 8) at 30 Hz and throws the other 8 away, then captures new frames and runs the whole network again. In the 3D view, the dots ahead of the gripper are the actions still to run before the next inference.

    a = \tfrac{1}{2}(\hat a + 1)\,(a_{\max} - a_{\min}) + a_{\min}
    \Delta t_{\text{replan}} = 8 \times \tfrac{1}{30}\,\text{s} \approx 0.27\,\text{s}
    8 × 33 ms = 0.27 s between observations

  1. One training step

    train_turbovla.py repeats this loop for 50,000 optimizer steps: draw a batch from simulation and real data, normalize it, predict a 16-step chunk, score it with a masked L1 loss, update the weights and fold them into a moving average. The average is what gets saved and run on the arm.

    \theta^{*} = \arg\min_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{(o, A) \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{mix}}}\ \mathcal{L}\big(\pi_\theta(o), A\big)

    o is the observation (images, instruction, state), A the recorded 16-step action chunk, and 𝒟mix the sim/real mixture.

  2. Mix simulation and real data

    Scripted bowl-stacking demonstrations from the MuJoCo scene, with randomized lighting, colours and poses, are concatenated with the real teleoperated set. DomainMixSampler draws each sample from the real set with probability r, independently of how large each set is, so the smaller real set is not drowned out.

    i \sim \begin{cases} \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}) & \text{with probability } r \\ \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{sim}}) & \text{with probability } 1 - r \end{cases}

    --real-ratio 0.5 by default; --no-sim trains on the real set alone. Indices are drawn with replacement from a generator seeded the same on every GPU and then split by rank.

    batch 16 per GPU · no gradient accumulation

  3. Collate and normalize

    The collator runs the DINOv3 image processor on every view, scales the state and the 16-step action chunk to [-1, 1], and keeps the action_is_pad flags for chunk steps that run past the end of an episode. The min-max ranges are merged across the two domains, so simulation and the real arm share one coordinate system.

    \tilde a_k = 2\,\frac{a_k - a_{\min}}{a_{\max} - a_{\min}} - 1,\quad k = 1, \dots, 16
    a_{\min} = \min\big(a_{\min}^{\text{sim}}, a_{\min}^{\text{real}}\big),\quad a_{\max} = \max\big(a_{\max}^{\text{sim}}, a_{\max}^{\text{real}}\big)
    m_k = 1 - \text{is\_pad}_k
    pixels B × V × 3 × 224 × 224 · state B × 6 · actions B × 16 × 6 · mask B × 16

  4. Forward pass

    The batch goes through the same network as in the Inference tab. BERT is frozen and receives no gradient; DINOv3, the projections, the interaction layers and the action head are all trained. With --precision bf16 the forward pass runs under autocast.

    \hat A = \pi_\theta\big(I, \ell, \tilde s\big) \in [-1, 1]^{16 \times 6}
    \theta = \theta_{\text{DINOv3}} \cup \theta_{\text{proj}} \cup \theta_{\text{interaction}} \cup \theta_{\text{head}}
    â · B × 16 × 6

  5. Masked L1 loss

    The absolute error is taken per chunk step and per joint. Padded steps are zeroed and the sum is divided by the number of valid entries, so a chunk cut short at the end of an episode is not penalized for steps that do not exist. --gripper-loss-weight scales the gripper column when the open/close timing matters more.

    \mathcal{L} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{16} \sum_{j=1}^{6} m_k\, w_j\, \lvert \hat a_{kj} - \tilde a_{kj} \rvert}{6 \sum_{k} m_k}
    w_j = 1 \ \text{for the five joints},\quad w_6 = w_{\text{grip}}

    --loss-type mse or smooth_l1 replace the absolute error with a squared or Huber error; the masking and the normalization stay the same.

  6. Optimizer step

    Gradients are clipped to a global norm of 1.0, then AdamW updates three parameter groups: the action head, the remaining trainable modules, and DINOv3, each at 5 × 10-5. The learning rate ramps up linearly over the first 1,000 steps and then follows a cosine down to 5 % of its base value at step 50,000.

    g \leftarrow g \cdot \min\!\big(1,\ 1 / \lVert g \rVert_2\big)
    \eta(u) = \eta_0 \begin{cases} \dfrac{u + 1}{1000} & u < 1000 \\[4pt] 0.05 + 0.95\,\dfrac{1 + \cos(\pi p)}{2} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
    p = \frac{u - 1000}{49\,000},\quad \eta_0 = 5 \times 10^{-5}

    u counts optimizer updates. Weight decay is 10-10, effectively off.

  7. Moving average and checkpoint

    After every update the script folds the new weights into an exponential moving average. The average changes slowly, which smooths out the noise of individual batches. Every 5,000 steps it writes the EMA weights, the raw weights, config.json (cameras, image size, fps, task) and stats_turbovla.json; the rollout script loads the EMA weights.

    \bar\theta \leftarrow 0.999\,\bar\theta + 0.001\,\theta
    \text{averaging window} \approx \frac{1}{1 - 0.999} = 1000 \text{ updates}

DINOv3 image tokens and a BERT-encoded instruction are fused by six bi-attention layers, and an ACT-style decoder predicts 16 actions, of which the NexArm runs 8 before looking again.

Architecture

TurboVLA keeps ACT’s idea of decoding a chunk of actions from learned queries, but replaces the encoder side. Images are encoded by a large self-supervised vision transformer, the task arrives as text, and the two are fused before the action head reads them. The chunk is short (16 actions) and only half of it is run, so the policy re-plans about every quarter second.

Part Setting used for the NexArm
Vision DINOv3 ViT-B/16, 224 × 224, views encoded separately, trainable
Vision projection LN → MLP 768 → 1024 → 256 + linear skip, LN, + view embedding
Language BERT-base uncased, frozen, Linear 768 → 256
Interaction 6 × (shared-logit bi-attention, 4 heads + text transformer)
State LN → MLP 6 → 256 → 512 → 2 tokens + learned positions
Action decoder 16 queries, 3 pre-norm layers, d 256, 8 heads, FFN 2048
Output MLP 256 → 512 → 512 → 6, tanh, min-max to joint units
Chunk / executed 16 / 8 actions

Training

The training script can mix two sources: scripted bowl-stacking demonstrations generated in the MuJoCo scene with domain randomization, and real teleoperated demonstrations. With --real-ratio 0.5 a sampler draws each training sample from the real set with probability one half, so the smaller real set is not drowned out by simulation.

Setting Default in train_turbovla.py
Data sim + real, or either alone (--no-sim for real only)
Batch 16 per device, no accumulation
Optimizer AdamW, lr 5e-5 for the head, interaction and DINOv3
Schedule 1,000 warm-up steps, cosine to 5 %, 50,000 steps, save every 5,000
Loss L1 over non-padded chunk steps, optional --gripper-loss-weight
Stability gradient clipping at 1.0, EMA of weights with decay 0.999
Starting point from scratch, or the released RoboTwin checkpoint

The checkpoint carries its own contract. config.json stores the camera keys in order, the image size, the fps and the default task prompt, and stats_turbovla.json stores the min-max ranges. The rollout script reads both, so a checkpoint trained without the top camera is run without it.

Evaluation status

Not yet measured

No physical success rate has been published yet. The training script logs the masked L1 loss, which does not say whether the arm actually stacks the bowls. The table and protocol below are the planned evaluation, not results.

Metric Value How it is measured
Stacking success rate — Fraction of trials that end with the bowls nested and released, judged from the front camera.
Sub-task success — Separate counts for grasp, lift, place and release, so a failure can be located.
Sim-to-real gap — Success in the MuJoCo scene minus success on the real arm for the same checkpoint and start poses.
Effect of co-training — Real-arm success with --real-ratio 0.5 against a real-data-only run of the same length.

Protocol

  1. Fixed bowl start poses drawn from the recorded workspace, the same list for every policy.
  2. Each trial starts from the robot’s ready pose with examples/nexarm/rollout_turbovla.py and the EMA checkpoint.
  3. A trial ends at success, at a collision, or after 60 seconds.
