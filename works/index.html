Blog

Interactive explainers and technical notes on how AI systems work under the hood.

  • mobile robots from scratch (2026, 15-part series) — a scroll-through course on building a wheeled robot that finds its own way, from wheels, encoders and motors through ESP32, Raspberry Pi and Jetson, lidar, ROS 2 and depth cameras to Kalman filters, SLAM and navigation, with every algorithm running live in a simulated room in your browser
    1. what a mobile robot is — the sense–think–act loop, the robot's pose, and the frames every wheeled robot keeps track of, worked through in a simulated room
    2. wheels and kinematics — how two wheel speeds become one motion of the robot, the centre it turns about, the sideways move it can't make, and the error of adding up motion in steps
    3. what a sensor is — the properties every sensor has, noise and bias, what averaging and filtering can and can't fix, an ultrasonic sensor's blind angles, and why a gyro or an accelerometer alone drifts away
    4. encoders and odometry — how a Hall-effect encoder counts wheel turns, how quadrature tells forward from back, how the counts become a pose, and why that pose drifts away from the truth
    5. motors and PID control — how a DC motor's speed follows its voltage, how PWM makes a voltage out of a switch, why a fixed command fails under load, and how the P, I and D terms of a PID controller make a wheel hold its speed
    6. the microcontroller, ESP32 — why a robot splits its work between a computer and a microcontroller, three ways an ESP32 can count encoder edges and when each fails, why a control loop must keep time, and how much a serial link to ROS 2 can carry
    7. the onboard computer, Raspberry Pi and Jetson — what a robot's main computer runs, how small delays add up between seeing an obstacle and reacting to it, what that costs in stopping distance, what happens when a program can't keep up, and how to choose between a Raspberry Pi and a Jetson
    8. lidar, how it measures — how a lidar measures distance with light, by timing a pulse, by triangulating with a camera, or by the phase of a modulated beam; why triangulation gets worse with the square of the distance; how a spinning head makes a scan; and the kinds of lidar there are
    9. lidar, processing a scan — what a robot does with a lidar scan, from a list of ranges to points, removing the smear a moving robot puts in a scan, finding walls as straight lines, and lining two scans up with ICP to measure how the robot moved
    10. ROS 2, how the pieces talk — what ROS 2 is and how a robot's programs pass messages, why sensor data is sent best effort and commands reliably, how deep a subscriber's queue should be, how the map and odom frames hide odometry's drift, and why every message carries the time it was measured
    11. depth cameras — how a depth camera measures a distance for every pixel, with two cameras, a projected pattern or the time light takes; why stereo needs texture and gets worse with the square of the distance; how pixels become a point cloud; and what a depth camera sees that a 2-D lidar can't
    12. sensor fusion with Kalman filters — how a Kalman filter combines two uncertain guesses into one better one, predicts and corrects as the robot moves, fuses odometry with measurements in the room as an extended Kalman filter, and why tuning its noise matters as much as its equations
    13. localisation with particle filters — how a robot finds where it is on a known map with no idea where it started, using hundreds of guesses weighed against each lidar scan; why a Kalman filter can't do that; how many guesses it takes; and how the filter recovers when the robot is picked up and moved
    14. SLAM — how a robot builds a map of a room it has never seen while working out where it is in it; occupancy grids and log-odds, why maps from odometry blur, how scan matching sharpens them, and how closing a loop with a pose graph removes the drift that is left
    15. path planning and navigation — how a robot gets from here to there on its map; costmaps and inflation, A* compared with Dijkstra, how much a planner should fear obstacles, following the plan with pure pursuit, replanning when the way is blocked, and how Nav2 puts it all together
  • deep learning for computer vision (2026, 6-part series) — a scroll-through course in deep learning for computer vision that follows Stanford's CS231n, from nearest neighbours to convolutional networks, with every model running live in your browser, on real CIFAR-10 photos and on small problems you can see whole
    1. image classification and nearest neighbours — a scroll-through walk through the data-driven approach and k-nearest neighbours, on real CIFAR-10 photos of planes, cars, frogs and ships
    2. linear classifiers and loss functions — a scroll-through walk through scores, templates, the SVM and softmax losses and regularisation, on CIFAR-10 photos and on points in the plane
    3. optimisation — a scroll-through walk across a loss landscape small enough to see whole, with random search, gradient descent, SGD, momentum and Adam each leaving its path
    4. backpropagation and neural networks — a scroll-through look at backpropagation on small computational graphs, then a two-layer network trained in your browser on a spiral that no linear classifier can split
    5. convolutional networks — a scroll-through look at convolution, a small filter sliding over a photo, with padding, stride, ReLU and max pooling, and a 1,460-parameter convnet set against part 2's 12,292-parameter linear classifier
    6. visualising and understanding — a scroll-through look inside a trained convnet, with class activation maps, occlusion and gradient maps, a sanity check with random weights, its mistakes, edited photos, and a nudge of 2/255 per pixel that fools it
  • clustering from scratch (2026, 4-part series) — a four-part, scroll-through tour of the classic clustering algorithms (k-means, hierarchical clustering, DBSCAN and Gaussian mixtures), run live on points you can change, with every step drawn and every number real
    1. k-means — a scroll-through, step-by-step walk through Lloyd's algorithm and k-means++, with every distance drawn and every number computed on the points you see
    2. hierarchical clustering — merge the two closest clusters, again and again, and read every possible clustering off one tree; with single, complete, average and Ward linkage run live
    3. DBSCAN and density — clusters as crowded regions, grown point by point from core points, with a real notion of noise; DBSCAN run live, with ε and minPts on sliders
    4. Gaussian mixtures and EM — clusters as overlapping, tilted Gaussian blobs, fitted by the EM algorithm; soft assignments, the log-likelihood that only goes up, and choosing k with BIC, all run live
  • attention is all you need (2026, 5-part series) — a five-part, scroll-through rebuild of the Transformer from the paper, from a single attention head to a trained model writing text with a KV cache, with every step drawn and every number real
    1. self-attention from scratch — a scroll-through, step-by-step walk through scaled dot-product attention, with every multiplication drawn and every number real
    2. multi-head attention and positions — how the Transformer learns word order from sine waves, and why it runs several attention heads side by side
    3. one encoder block — residual connections, layer normalisation and the feed-forward network, the parts that turn one attention layer into a Transformer encoder block
    4. the decoder, masking and training — how the Transformer's decoder writes one word at a time, why it needs a mask, how it reads the encoder through cross-attention, and how the whole model is trained
    5. inference and the KV cache — how a trained Transformer writes fast: the KV cache drawn row by row, multi-query and grouped-query attention, the attention patterns models use, and what it all costs in memory
  • how a language model picks the next word (2026) — an interactive look at tokens, scores, softmax, temperature and sampling, the loop behind every chatbot reply
