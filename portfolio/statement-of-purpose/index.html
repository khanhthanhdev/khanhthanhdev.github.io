Statement of Purpose

Research direction and motivation for the VinRobotics Talent Program Internship.

My goal is to become a research engineer and, eventually, a PhD researcher building adaptive assistive robots. I am drawn to robotics because the useful questions are rarely isolated to perception, policy learning, or hardware alone. A system has to make decisions from incomplete observations, act through imperfect mechanisms, and still be evaluated in a way that another person can inspect and trust. I want to work on reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and learning from demonstration because they offer a practical route from human behavior to robust robot behavior, while keeping the gap between a promising result and a physical system visible.

PRISM, a research project on evaluating AI peer reviewers, first made that discipline concrete for me. The project asks whether a generated review is genuinely useful rather than merely plausible. My contribution involved an evidence-grounded validation workflow: explicit dataset contracts, blinded annotation flows, cached judge inputs, statistical reporting, and offline synthetic tests when the full research corpus was unavailable. That work taught me to preserve provenance, report what a test did and did not establish, and treat unsupported conclusions as bugs in the research process. Those habits transfer directly to robot learning, where a policy result is only meaningful when the data, action contract, evaluation seeds, and failure cases are clear.

I have been applying that mindset to NexArm, an existing LeRobot/Hiwonder fork to which I contributed robot-learning infrastructure. The work did not begin with a claim about a new policy. It began with making the environment usable: mapping the six-action servo contract to MuJoCo controls, keeping detailed CAD meshes visual while using stable primitive contact geometry, adding front and wrist camera observations, and checking that the documented open command actually increased jaw separation. I then worked on scripted demonstration generation, dataset metadata and provenance, Rerun-based inspection, ACT-compatible rollout support, and focused tests for action conversion, contacts, cameras, and pick-and-place behavior. A deterministic scripted task accepted 10 of 10 seeded trials after a collision-geometry repair; that is evidence that the scripted simulation path works, not a claim of learned-policy performance.

For the immediate future, I am seeking the VinRobotics Talent Program Internship as a research-engineering environment where I can deepen this work under researchers who connect embodied machine learning to physical validation. My primary interest is RL, imitation learning, and LfD: collecting or generating useful demonstrations, establishing representations and action interfaces that survive across tools, training policies such as ACT when the data supports them, and evaluating the result on fixed, interpretable tasks. Teleoperation and MuJoCo are supporting areas for me because they make those questions tractable. They let a team inspect demonstrations, reproduce a reset, test a failure mode, and decide what must be measured on hardware before claiming progress.

VinRobotics is especially compelling because the Physical Intelligence Lab’s Embodied ML direction treats simulation and real robots as connected parts of the research loop. I want to contribute as someone who is comfortable moving between a research question and the engineering needed to test it. The next skills I need are stronger foundations in modern policy learning, systematic sim-to-real evaluation, and the research judgment to decide when an experiment is ready for hardware. Working with a team that validates ideas through both simulation and real systems would make those lessons concrete.

My experience at STEAM for Vietnam adds a complementary perspective. Building software for robotics competition operations has meant thinking about people who need the system to remain understandable under time pressure: event staff coordinating shared state, local networks that cannot rely on connectivity, and rules that must be represented precisely. FIRST Global Challenge 2023 gave that interest an earlier source. Together, they have made collaboration and operational reliability central to how I approach technical work.

I selected this CV, cover letter, and LinkedIn profile as a coherent package rather than as copies of the same document. The CV gives a reviewer a compact, evidence-first view of the robot-learning work, research objective, skills, and awards. The cover letter makes the specific case for VinRobotics and connects my contribution boundary to the internship’s embodied-ML setting. LinkedIn provides the public, discoverable version of the same direction, with links to NexArm, PRISM, and this portfolio. Each artifact serves a different reading context, but all should make the same promise: I will bring research rigor, careful implementation, and an honest account of what has been verified.

In the long term, I intend to pursue a PhD and a research career focused on adaptive human–robot collaboration, particularly assistive systems that learn from people while remaining safe to evaluate and improve. The VinRobotics internship is the right next step because it would let me learn how a serious robotics lab turns demonstrations, policies, simulations, and hardware evidence into research that deserves to be trusted.
